Theory & Concepts (For Everyone)

What is Value-at-Risk (VaR)?

"What's the worst-case loss I could face in 1 day at a 1% confidence level?"

VaR answers this fundamental question for portfolio managers, risk officers, and regulators.

Example: A $1M portfolio with 1-day 1% VaR of $25K means: there's a 99% chance the portfolio won't lose more than $25K tomorrow.

Who uses it? Investment banks, hedge funds, insurance companies, pension funds, and central banks.

Why It Matters

Risk Management: Set trading limits and position sizes

Regulation: Basel III requires banks to calculate VaR for capital requirements

Decision-Making: CFOs use VaR for capital allocation and strategic planning

Client Trust: Transparent communication of investment risks

Beta (β) Sensitivity

Measures how a stock/portfolio moves relative to the market:

  • β = 1.0: Moves exactly with market
  • β = 1.6: Moves 60% MORE than market (aggressive)
  • β = 0.5: Moves 50% LESS than market (defensive)

In this project: US stocks (β=1.6) are more aggressive than German stocks (β=1.3)

EWMA: Smart Volatility Tracking

Exponentially Weighted Moving Average gives more weight to recent data while maintaining historical context.

λ = 0.94 means:

  • 94% weight to recent trends
  • 6% weight to historical patterns
  • Result: Captures volatility spikes during crises

Methodology: Multiple Approaches

📊 Parametric VaR (Normal Distribution)

Approach: Assumes returns follow a "bell curve"

Advantages: Fast, mathematically elegant, requires less data

Limitation: Real markets have "fat tails" (more extreme events than predicted)

Use Case: Routine risk estimation, regulatory reporting

📈 Historical VaR (Empirical)

Approach: Uses actual historical returns; no assumptions about distribution

Advantages: Captures real behavior including extreme events

Limitation: Can't predict unprecedented events; relies on past patterns

Use Case: Validation, stress-testing, robustness checks

Why Use Both Methods?

Parametric VaR is elegant but makes assumptions. Historical VaR is robust but limited by past data. Using both provides:

  • Validation: Do the two approaches agree?
  • Robustness: Which method is more appropriate for the current regime?
  • Confidence: Risk estimates are independent of methodology

Data & Real-World Market Events

This analysis spans 5 years of market data, encompassing two major global crises that stress-test the risk model.

Sep 2017 - Feb 2020

Baseline Period

Normal market conditions. Establishes baseline volatility and correlation estimates for the portfolio.

March 2020

COVID-19 Pandemic Shock

Sudden market shock with extreme volatility. NASDAQ100 and DAX both declined sharply. VaR estimates spike as diversification breaks down during crisis.

Feb 2022

Russia-Ukraine Conflict

Geopolitical shock with severe currency impact. USD/RUB and EUR/RUB depreciate 30%+ within weeks. Portfolio value in rubles increases due to currency effect overwhelming equity losses.

Sep 2022

Analysis Date

Final analysis captures full crisis impact. Demonstrates how portfolio risk changed during extreme stress periods.
