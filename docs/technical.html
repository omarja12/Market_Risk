Risk Decomposition

The portfolio return is decomposed into equity and foreign exchange components:

$$ r_t^{RUB} = \omega_1 \beta_1 y_{1t}^{EUR} + \omega_2 \beta_2 y_{2t}^{USD} + r_t^{EUR/RUB} + r_t^{USD/RUB} $$

Where:

  • \(\omega_i\) = weight of asset i (0.6 for US, 0.4 for German)
  • \(\beta_i\) = beta (systematic risk) of asset i
  • \(y_{it}\) = market return of asset i
  • \(r_t^{FX}\) = foreign exchange return

Volatility Estimation - EWMA

Exponentially Weighted Moving Average with smoothing parameter λ = 0.94:

$$ \sigma_t^2 = (1 - \lambda) r_{t-1}^2 + \lambda \sigma_{t-1}^2 $$

This recursive formula emphasizes recent observations (6% weight) while maintaining historical context (94% weight).

Advantages:

  • Captures volatility clustering around market shocks
  • Responds quickly to market changes
  • Maintains stability during normal periods
  • Computationally efficient

Parametric VaR

Assumes normally distributed returns. The 1% VaR is calculated as:

$$ VaR_{1\%} = \mu - \sigma \times Z_{0.99} $$

Where:

  • \(\mu\) = mean return
  • \(\sigma\) = standard deviation (volatility)
  • \(Z_{0.99}\) = 99th percentile of standard normal (2.326)

Characteristics:

  • Mathematically elegant and fast
  • Requires only mean and variance
  • Underestimates tail risk during crises
  • Standard for regulatory reporting (Basel III)

Historical VaR

Identifies the α-th percentile from empirical distribution of sorted historical returns:

$$ VaR_\alpha = \text{sorted returns} \left[ \lceil (1-\alpha) \times n \rceil \right] $$

Where n = number of historical observations

Characteristics:

  • No distributional assumptions
  • Captures actual market behavior
  • More accurate for tail events
  • Limited by historical data availability

10-Day VaR Scaling

Assuming i.i.d. returns, 10-day VaR scales from 1-day VaR:

$$ VaR_{10-day} = VaR_{1-day} \times \sqrt{10} $$

This relationship holds under normality and independence assumptions.

Data Processing

Log Returns Calculation:

$$ r_t = \ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right) $$

Log returns are used throughout (continuous compounding).

Portfolio Return:

$$ r_P = \omega_1 r_1 + \omega_2 r_2 + \ldots + \omega_n r_n $$

Weighted sum of component returns.

Covariance Matrix

The covariance matrix captures correlation dynamics between risk factors:

$$ \Sigma = E[(r - \mu)(r - \mu)^T] $$

Estimated using EWMA method to capture time-varying correlations.

Key Observations:

  • Pre-crisis correlations: 0.3-0.5
  • Crisis correlations: 0.7-0.9
  • Diversification benefit reduces during market stress

Validation & Testing

Backtesting:

  • Analysis during major market events (COVID, Ukraine)

Assumption Testing:

  • Normality testing of returns (Jarque-Bera test)
  • Correlation stability analysis
  • Stress testing with historical shocks
