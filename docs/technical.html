Risk decomposition

Portfolio return is decomposed into equity and foreign exchange components:

$$r_t^{RUB} = \omega_1 \beta_1 y_{1t}^{EUR} + \omega_2 \beta_2 y_{2t}^{USD} + r_t^{EUR/RUB} + r_t^{USD/RUB}$$
  • $\omega_i$ — weight of asset i (0.6 US, 0.4 German)
  • $\beta_i$ — systematic risk (1.6 US, 1.3 German)
  • $y_{it}$ — market return of asset i
  • $r_t^{FX}$ — foreign exchange return

EWMA volatility

Exponentially weighted moving average with smoothing parameter λ = 0.94:

$$\sigma_t^2 = (1 - \lambda) r_{t-1}^2 + \lambda \sigma_{t-1}^2$$

Recent observations get 6% weight, historical data retains 94% weight — enough to react to shocks without overfitting to noise.

Why EWMA

  • Captures volatility clustering around shocks
  • Responds quickly to regime changes
  • Computationally efficient, no lookback window to tune

Parametric VaR

Assumes normally distributed returns:

$$VaR_{1\%} = \mu - \sigma \times Z_{0.99}$$
  • $\mu$ — mean return
  • $\sigma$ — standard deviation
  • $Z_{0.99}$ — 99th percentile of standard normal (2.326)

Trade-offs

  • Fast, requires only mean and variance
  • Underestimates tail risk during crises
  • Basel III's regulatory standard

Historical VaR

Empirical percentile taken from sorted historical returns — no distributional assumption:

$$VaR_\alpha = \text{sorted returns}\left[\lceil (1-\alpha)\times n \rceil\right]$$
  • Captures actual market behavior, including fat tails
  • More accurate for tail events than the parametric method
  • Limited by the depth of available history

10-day scaling

Assuming i.i.d. returns, the 1-day VaR scales to a 10-day horizon by the square root of time:

$$VaR_{10\text{-}day} = VaR_{1\text{-}day} \times \sqrt{10}$$

Log returns

Used throughout for continuous compounding:

$$r_t = \ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right)$$

Covariance matrix

Captures correlation dynamics between risk factors, estimated with the same EWMA approach:

$$\Sigma = E[(r - \mu)(r - \mu)^T]$$

The correlation between equity and forex returns is negative throughout the sample (roughly +0.3 to −0.8) and moves further negative during crises — a currency effect that partly hedges equity losses for a ruble-based investor, rather than the positive, diversification-eroding correlation sometimes assumed by default.
