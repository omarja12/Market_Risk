What is Value-at-Risk

VaR answers one question: what's the worst-case loss at a given confidence level?

A $1M portfolio with a 1-day 1% VaR of $30K means there's a 99% chance it won't lose more than $30K the next trading day.

Who relies on it

  • Investment banks — setting trading desk risk limits
  • Hedge funds — sizing positions within risk budgets
  • Insurers — modeling liabilities against investments
  • Regulators — enforcing Basel III capital requirements

Key findings

Numbers below are recomputed directly from the notebook's own logic run against the real data — see the Results page for the charts.

Volatility dynamics

  • Equity EWMA volatility spiked from a ~15–25% baseline to ~106% annualized during COVID-19
  • Forex volatility spiked again in Feb–Mar 2022, briefly exceeding its COVID peak (~73% annualized)
  • Mean reversion during recovery phases

VaR estimates

  • 1-day 1% parametric VaR jumped from ~4.1% (23 Feb 2022) to ~10.1% (9 Mar 2022) as EWMA reacted to the FX shock
  • Historical VaR barely moved over the same window (~4.2% → ~4.4%) — it reflects the full return history, not just the latest shock
  • 10-day VaR scales by exactly √10 over the 1-day figure (a property of the model, not an independent finding)

Correlation dynamics

  • Equity–forex correlation is negative throughout the sample (roughly +0.3 to −0.8), not positive
  • It turns more negative during both crises — a currency depreciation partly offset equity losses instead of adding to them
  • This is a natural hedge for a ruble-based investor holding foreign equities, not a diversification breakdown

Market events analyzed

COVID-19 — FEB–MAR 2020

NASDAQ fell ~28%, DAX fell ~39% peak-to-trough. Equity volatility spiked ~4x; the equity–forex correlation dropped toward −0.8.

RUSSIA-UKRAINE — 23 FEB TO 9 MAR 2022

DAX fell 5%, NASDAQ was roughly flat, while USD/RUB and EUR/RUB depreciated 38–48%. Because equity and forex returns are negatively correlated, the portfolio's ruble value rose ~30–50% despite the shock.
