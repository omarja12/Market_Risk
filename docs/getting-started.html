FAQ

Q: What does VaR actually measure?
A: VaR measures maximum expected loss at a given confidence level over a specific time period. "99% confident we won't lose more than X in 1 day."
Q: Why compare parametric and historical VaR?
A: Parametric VaR is fast but assumes normal distribution. Historical VaR is robust to extreme events. Comparing them validates findings and reveals assumption violations.
Q: What does β = 1.6 mean?
A: The asset is 60% more volatile than the overall market. If market moves 10%, this asset moves ~16% on average.
Q: Why EWMA and not simple average?
A: EWMA gives more weight to recent data, capturing volatility spikes during crises. Simple average treats old data equally, missing current market conditions.
Q: What does 1-day vs 10-day VaR mean?
A: 1-day VaR: maximum loss tomorrow. 10-day VaR: maximum loss over 10 trading days. Usually scaled by √10 assuming independence.
Q: Why does diversification fail in 2020 & 2022?
A: During crises, all assets correlate highly (correlations 0.7-0.9 vs. 0.3-0.5 normally). Flight-to-safety removes diversification benefits.
Q: Can I modify the code?
A: Yes. The notebook is self-contained with clear sections. You can change parameters, add analysis, or test different time periods.
Q: How do I validate results?
A: Compare parametric vs. historical VaR, test across different periods, stress-test with known market events, check normality assumptions.
